# Synthetic Demonstration Investor Deck Content

> **SYNTHETIC DEMO — NOT CUSTOMER EVIDENCE.** This deck demonstrates the interview-analysis and pilot-selection pipeline with hand-authored hypothetical records. It must not be used to represent customer traction, pricing validation, revenue, market demand, or investment evidence.

## Cover

**NACA Airfoil Kit Pro — Synthetic Discovery Demonstration**
**Illustrative pipeline only; not customer evidence**

## Slide 1

### What This Demonstration Does—and Does Not Prove

- Demonstrates a structured path from interview notes to coding, qualification, and pilot decisions.
- Uses exactly 10 hand-authored illustrative records; no customer, prospect, transcript, survey, or CRM data is included.
- Proves only that the analytical workflow can be executed; it does not prove pain, demand, willingness to pay, conversion, or revenue.

## Slide 2

### The Synthetic Input Mirrors a Real Discovery Schema

- Each record includes segment, role, recent decision timeline, workflow frequency, pain severity, readiness, buyer path, price reaction, objection, and follow-up status.
- The schema forces a distinction between observed facts, quoted reactions, missing information, and interviewer confidence.
- The same template accepts a future real 10-interview dataset without mixing it with this demo.

## Slide 3

### Illustrative Price Reactions Require Behavioral Context

- Synthetic coding produces 3 `accept`, 2 `conditional_accept`, 1 `consider`, 1 `no_budget`, 2 `not_discussed`, and 1 `too_high` entries.
- `accept` and `conditional_accept` must remain separate: a condition may be scope, security, timing, or budget approval.
- `not_discussed` records must not be silently treated as negative or positive conversion outcomes.

**Footer:** SYNTHETIC DEMO — not a conversion rate or pricing result.

## Slide 4

### Objections Drive the Follow-Up, Not a Feature Roadmap

- In this illustrative set, budget appears 3 times; trust/accuracy, privacy, scope mismatch, and timing also appear.
- Each objection is linked to a follow-up action: budget path, scope boundary, evidence review, data-handling clarification, or nurture trigger.
- The workflow prevents a small sample from becoming an unsupported “top customer request” claim.

**Footer:** All counts are hand-authored examples.

## Slide 5

### Qualification Uses Gates Before Scores

- Mandatory gates cover screening-only scope, minimum data readiness, and operating commitment.
- Six dimensions score from 0 to 2: problem repetition, ownership, data readiness, pain/urgency, product fit, and collaboration.
- A total of 8/12 indicates eligibility; 10/12 indicates priority, subject to mandatory gates.

## Slide 6

### A Synthetic Cohort Shows Capacity Discipline

- The illustrative ranking produces six priority candidates, but the cohort capacity is limited to five.
- The five selected records have the strongest score/readiness combination; the sixth becomes a reserve.
- The example shows why capacity and tie-break rules should be documented before commercial outreach.

**Footer:** Candidate labels, scores, and offers are fictional demonstration data.

## Slide 8

### The Real Milestone Is Replacing Synthetic Data

- Upload or enter the real ten-interview records using the anonymized intake template.
- Re-run the pipeline in a separate real-data directory, with source and completeness checks.
- Replace every synthetic chart, score, and statement before any investor, pricing, or product decision is made.

**Closing statement:** A transparent pipeline is valuable only when it is eventually populated with real evidence.
